01 — Who We Serve

Defining a Hobby-X collector.

Think Funko Pops, Hot Toys figures, Gunpla model kits, Jordan drops, and Pokémon cards — physical objects tied to a fandom, released in limited runs, that people actively hunt, display and trade. We call this space "Hobby-X": any hobby built around collecting branded, IP-driven objects rather than general merchandise.

We selected these six categories because their collectors are highly active, fueling multi-billion-dollar markets backed by well-known brands and vibrant communities — unlike traditional collectibles like stamps or coins, these hobbies create thriving, brand-centric economies with constant new releases to chase.

Action Figures
Designer Toys & Blind Boxes
Model Kits & Lego
Diecast & Scale Model Cars
Sneakers (collector-grade, drop-driven)
Trading Cards (collectible / hybrid grade)
Why this matters for a business model
Collectors buy to build and grow a collection — not to consume. Unlike a casual shopper who buys once, a collector keeps buying as new drops release, keeps documenting what they own, and keeps engaging with the community around it. That recurring, high-intent behavior is what makes this audience valuable — not a single transaction, but a habit.

02 — Current Landscape

Collectors are thriving — but fragmented without Scorred.

India's collector economy is already active across multiple touchpoints — it's just operating in silos, with no single app owning the collector's daily habit the way Instagram owns photo-sharing or Strava owns running.

Social Communities
10K–100K+ follower IG pages, FB/Reddit/Discord groups, hundreds of WhatsApp & Telegram groups. Engagement is strong — but scattered.
Specialty Retailers
Dedicated importers (InABox, FatCat, Superhero Toy Store) and WhatsApp/IG-only stores. Commerce is active — engagement is limited.
Ecommerce Platforms
Amazon & Flipkart — a transactional presence, without any hobby focus.
Comic Con & Events
11 cities, 200K+ attendees, plus local meetups. Real enthusiasm, limited by frequency.

What's missing is a unified digital layer that connects community, commerce, and discovery.

03 — The Problem

Fragmentation costs collectors information, trust, and buyers.

Because no platform owns this space end-to-end, the same three problems show up everywhere a collector turns — whether they're trying to learn about a new drop, sell a piece from their shelf, or get noticed by the brand they're obsessed with.

Fragmented information
Releases, reviews, features and events are spread across Instagram, Facebook, blogs, WhatsApp and seller channels — no centralized place to look.
Buying & selling friction
Private messaging, negotiation and price discovery are ad hoc, with trust a major concern. Sellers struggle to reach the right buyers efficiently.
No brand-collector layer
Brands have minimal direct connection with collectors — limited opportunity for engagement, feedback, or exclusive releases.
