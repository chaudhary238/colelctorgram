1. Purpose & scope

Three flows had accumulated structural problems: the item page existed twice, adding an item demanded information users rarely have to hand, and the listing page also existed twice — one of the duplicates crashing on load. This addendum specifies the corrected behaviour.

In scope: database item page, collection item page, add-to-collection, collection completeness, listing detail, price fairness, feed post types.
Out of scope: chat, communities, events, XP economy beyond the two rules stated in §5.

2. Guiding principles

Three principles were applied throughout and should govern future work in these areas.

PrincipleRequirementReason
One page per conceptA concept has exactly one implementation. Viewer differences are expressed as variations within it, never as separate screens.Both duplicated pages had silently drifted apart, and one had crashed unnoticed.
Vertical space is a budgetLong sections collapse by default. A page's resting height must not scale with its content volume.Item pages grew unboundedly with catalogue copy and discussion.
No unbacked dataNo figure, badge or label may appear unless the product measurably tracks it.Deal counts, a condition ladder and verification tiers were all displayed but never recorded.

3. Item page — revises §9.4, §9.5

The database entry and the collection item are one page. Viewing a catalogue entry, your own copy, or another collector's copy differs by one card and one button.

3.1 Page structure (normative order)

  1. Photo — the owner's if present, otherwise the catalogue reference
  2. Provenance line — review state and contributor, one line
  3. Title, brand, scale, year
  4. Estimated value — suppressed when the ownership card carries a private price
  5. Ownership slot — the only variable region
  6. Community stats — owners and wishlist counts, one row
  7. About (collapsible, contains specifications)
  8. Rating — always expanded, last section
  9. Comments
  10. Action bar

3.2 Ownership slot requirements

RefCaseBehaviour
ITM-01Catalogue onlyNo ownership card. Estimated value visible. Action: Add to collection.
ITM-02Owned, by ownerCollapsible card, collapsed by default. Header About your copy; summary line shows status, condition, price paid. Expanded: status, condition, price paid, photo count, listed state.
ITM-03Owned, by visitorSame card and geometry. Header In @handle's collection. Price row omitted. Adds Ask @handle about it.
ITM-04Pre-orderSame card, gold tint, collapsed by default. Summary line carries expected date and balance due. Expanded: order date (if present), expected, seller, total, deposit, balance.
ITM-05ListedA row inside the ownership card linking to the live listing. A second price block must never appear.
ITM-06IncompleteCard opens expanded. Summary line names what is missing. Empty rows are tappable, leading to the two-field editor.
ITM-07Contributed entryNo ownership card; the provenance line carries attribution. Estimated value remains visible.

Rationale — one card, not two

Pre-orders previously rendered a separate timeline card and an ownership card, duplicating content. Folding the timeline into the ownership card as its expanded body keeps one mental model — "this is my copy" — with the gold tint carrying the distinction instead of a second container.

3.3 Rating

The rating section must display the community average as a rendered value, not only as text. Requirement: numeric score, a star meter filled to the true fraction (4.5 renders as four and a half stars), the rating count, and the user's own tappable stars — all within one section, roughly two lines tall.

4. Adding to a collection — revises §8.1

Previously a single + opened a form requesting photos, condition, price and notes. Collectors adding a shelf of items rarely hold that information at that moment, producing abandoned adds or invented values.

RefRequirementBehaviour
ADD-01Quick addA single tap adds the item with no intervening screen. The user remains in context and may add several items consecutively.
ADD-02Default statusOwned. Pre-order is correctable in one tap from the item menu.
ADD-03Wishlist interactionA wishlisted item must remain quick-addable as owned. Only an existing owned or pre-ordered copy blocks the add.
ADD-04Reward disclosureThe confirmation states the reward earned and the reward available: +5 XP · Added to collection / +20 XP when you add condition & price.
ADD-05Duplicate copiesThe same catalogue item may exist multiple times in one collection (e.g. one owned, one on pre-order). Each copy is independently addressable and editable.

5. Completeness & incentives

Missing data matters for one reason: the portfolio value is wrong. The pressure to complete therefore targets that number specifically, and the required set is kept minimal so the request carries authority.

5.1 Definition of complete

StatusRequiredOptional (permanently)
OwnedCondition, price paidPhotos, notes, acquisition date
Pre-orderExpected date, total priceDeposit, order date, seller

"Not announced" is a valid answer for an expected date and must not count as a gap.

5.2 Incentive requirements

RefRequirementBehaviour
CMP-01Value privacyPortfolio value is private until every owned item is complete. Visitors see the slot present but empty, labelled Value not shared. The rest of the profile stays public.
CMP-02Owner disclosureThe owner sees the true value with a lock indicator and a persistent card stating the completion count, the XP available, and a single action to resolve it.
CMP-03Per-item markerAn incomplete item's value line is replaced by the missing field named as an action — Add price, Add condition, Add details. No badge layer is added.
CMP-04Batch completionOne screen, one item at a time, two fields only, with skip and a progress indicator. Six items must be completable in under a minute.
CMP-05Reward5 XP on add, 20 XP on completion. Status corrections earn nothing.

Rationale — three rejected alternatives

  • Hiding the whole portfolio. An all-or-nothing penalty for one missing field, applied to the user's primary social asset. Encourages abandonment, and penalises a 200-item collection with three gaps more than a 4-item collection with four.
  • "Incomplete" badges per tile. Fourteen simultaneous warnings reads as a malfunctioning app and trains users to ignore the signal. Naming the specific missing field is quieter and actionable.
  • Excluding incomplete items from rank. Considered as loss framing, rejected as disproportionate while rank feeds real perks.

6. Listing page — revises §9.9

Two implementations existed: one for catalogue-seeded listings, one for user-created listings. The same listing rendered differently by entry point, and the user-created path referenced undeclared state, so a seller opening their own listing saw a blank screen.

RefRequirementBehaviour
LST-01Single implementationAll sources normalise to one shape and render one page. Market, collection item and profile entry points are visually identical.
LST-02Tab structureTab 1 Selling terms (default): condition, quantity where greater than one, origin, shipping, returns, trades. Tab 2 About the item: catalogue facts read from the database entry, with a link to it.
LST-03No duplicationShipping appears once. Catalogue attributes appear only in tab 2.
LST-04Q&ACollapsible, with question count in the header.
LST-05Removed elementsOrder card (quantity stepper, delivery toggle, estimated total), condition ladder, watcher count, deal counts. None were functional or tracked.
LST-06Pre-order exclusionA pre-ordered copy is not in hand and must never present as listed — no tag, no listing row, no inclusion in listed counts.

7. Price fairness signal

Purpose: give a seller evidence on whether to adjust their price. It is a seller-facing analytic collected from buyers, not a public rating.

AudienceSeesReason
VisitorThree options; after voting, confirmation of their own choice with the ability to change it. No aggregate figures at any point.Published votes would anchor every subsequent buyer's expectation and function as a public verdict on the seller.
SellerDistribution bar with percentages, a plain-language recommendation derived from the leading response, and a direct action to adjust the price.A chart is not a decision. The card must state what to do about it.

PRC-01 Votes are anonymous; the seller sees distribution only, never identity.
PRC-02 The seller receives a notification when a vote is cast.
PRC-03 One vote per user per listing, changeable and revocable.

8. Feed & post types — revises §8.5

RefRequirementBehaviour
FED-01No type pillsPost-type labels (Showcase, Discussion, Review, Poll, ISO) are internal taxonomy and must not render. A post's type is evident from its content.
FED-02Wanted post parityA wanted post uses the same card shell, author line and action row as any other post. No distinct border, tint or watermark.
FED-03Vocabulary"ISO" is internal. User-facing surfaces read "Wanted".

9. Data model changes

ChangeDetail
Copy identityCollection copies are keyed by copy identifier, not catalogue code, since a collection may hold the same item several times with different statuses.
Listing resolutionA single resolver answers "is this person's copy listed?", scoped to owner and status. The per-item listed flag is no longer authoritative.
Expected datesStored structurally as precision plus parts (month/quarter/year/not-announced), never as display text, so the pre-order calendar can bucket them. One shared formatter renders them.
Deferred detailsLater-supplied information is stored as a patch against the copy identifier and layered over the base record on read.
Retired fieldsDeal counts, condition ladder, ownership verification tiers, user-facing catalogue codes.

10. Defects closed

All were found by walking the flows end to end rather than by inspection.

Blocking — two crashes

  • Condition selection. Condition options are structured records and were rendered directly as labels, blanking the screen for any owned item in the completion flow.
  • Month selection. Month names were scoped to the calendar component and unreachable elsewhere, so choosing month precision on a pre-order blanked the application.
DefectCauseResolution
Listed tag on unlisted itemsThe tag read a flag on the shared catalogue record, so it appeared on every collector's copy.One resolver drives the tag, tap target, filter count and item page.
Pre-orders presenting as for saleListing lookup matched catalogue code only; a pre-ordered copy inherited the owned copy's listing.Resolution is scoped by status; pre-orders cannot present as listed.
Wrong copy opening; edits collidingAll lookups keyed by catalogue code.Keyed by copy identifier throughout.
Search returning no resultsInterest-derived category filter also applied to search.Search spans the catalogue unless filters are set explicitly.
Quick add silently refusingDuplicate guard counted wishlist rows as owned.Only an owned or pre-ordered copy blocks the add.
All pre-orders showing "TBD"Structured dates read as plain strings.Shared formatter; explicit "not announced" recognised as an answer.
Seller's own listing emptyAn item flagged as listed with no corresponding listing record.Listing records added, including one owned by another collector to exercise the visitor path.
Share sheet clippedFull-width sheet with horizontal padding and no border-box sizing, rendering wider than the viewport.Border-box sizing; icon strip given room for its labels.
Deal counts persistingDisplayed in four separate surfaces beyond the trust row.Removed from all; replaced with city or vouch counts.

11. Open decisions

Requires product sign-off

  1. Saved vocabulary. Listing pages say "watchlist" while filters say "Saved". One term should govern.
  2. Legacy listings. Listings without a catalogue code cannot link to a database entry. Resolves naturally as listings are recreated through the current flow; confirm no migration is required.
  3. Date precision. Whether pre-orders need day-level precision in the completion flow, or whether month and quarter suffice.
  4. Acquisition date. Currently optional and never requested. Confirm it stays a power-user field rather than entering the required set.

12. Acceptance criteria

  1. A catalogue entry and an owned copy of the same item present identical shared content; only the ownership card and action button differ.
  2. A visitor to a collection sees the same page as the owner, less the price row, plus a contact action.
  3. Three consecutive quick-adds require three taps and no intervening screens.
  4. A pre-ordered item shows no listing indication on any surface.
  5. Portfolio value is inaccessible to visitors while any owned item is incomplete, and the owner is told exactly how many items and how much XP remain.
  6. One listing opened from three entry points renders identically.
  7. A visitor never sees price-vote aggregates; the seller always sees a recommendation alongside them.
  8. No post-type label renders anywhere in the feed.
  9. No figure appears on any surface that the product does not track.
