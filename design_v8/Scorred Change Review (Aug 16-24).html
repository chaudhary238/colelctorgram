Scorred: what changed since 16 August

Five areas were rebuilt: the item page, how items get into a collection, the listing page, community moderation, and events. This document sets the previous behaviour beside the current build so each change can be checked on the call.

1. The item page

The database entry and a collection item were two separate screens that had drifted apart over time — different stat rows, different section order, different actions. They are now the same page. Differences between viewers amount to one card and one button, not two designs.

ElementBeforeNow
Page identityTwo components — database vs collection — which had diverged.One component serves both. Shared content is identical by construction, not by copy-paste.
Your copyOwnership details mixed into the page body.One collapsible card under the title: About your copy · Owned · Sealed · ₹4,800. Price visible only to you.
Pre-orderA separate gold timeline card plus the ownership card — two cards, overlapping content.Folded into the same card with a gold tint. Collapsed line shows ETA and balance due; inside are order date, total, deposit, balance.
Listed itemCompeting price blocks on one screen.A row inside the ownership card: Sale · ₹5,400 · View listing →. Never a second price block.

Community statsTwo large numeric tiles, ~80px for two numbers. Labelled "own" / "want".One compact row: 301 own this | 178 wishlisted, star icon to match the rest of the app.
AboutAlways fully expanded; long entries pushed the page down.Collapsible with Read more, and absorbed the specs grid.
Rating"4.5 avg · 34 ratings" in small grey text — the score was stated but never shown.A score block: large 4.7 /5, gold stars filled to the true fraction, rating count, then your own stars beside it.

Net effect

Roughly 200px of vertical space recovered above the fold, and the page ends just below the fold regardless of how much catalogue copy or discussion an item has.

2. Getting items into a collection New flow

Previously, adding one item opened a full form asking for price, condition and photos — information a collector rarely has to hand at the moment of adding. The result was either abandoned adds or invented data. The new flow separates adding from describing.

StepBeforeNow
AddingTap + → a form with photos, condition, price, notes. One item at a time.Tap + → the item is in, no screen. Several items can be added in a row.
Teaching the rewardNo indication that XP existed or how to earn more.The toast teaches it when it's earned: +5 XP · Added to collection / +20 XP when you add condition & price.
What "complete" meansEverything the old form asked for.Two fields only — condition and price paid (pre-order: ETA and total). Photos, notes and order dates stay optional permanently.
Portfolio valueAlways public, and wrong whenever items lacked prices.Private until complete. Visitors see Value not shared · —; you see the real number with a lock and a count of what's missing.
Incomplete itemsNo indication anything was missing.The tile's value line becomes Add price → / Add condition → / Add details → in gold — the gap shown where the data belongs.
FinishingRe-open the full item page per item.Finish your items: one item at a time, two fields, Save & next, skip allowed, progress bar. Six items in under a minute.

Design decisions worth noting

  • Quick-add still earns XP. If adding earned nothing, quick-add would feel like a trap that generates homework. The action is rewarded; finishing is rewarded more.
  • The portfolio is not hidden — only the value is withheld, and only because we genuinely can't compute it. Hiding the whole portfolio punishes one missing field and encourages abandonment.
  • No "Incomplete" badges. Fourteen loud badges reads like a broken app. Naming the missing thing in its own slot is both quieter and more actionable.
  • Status is correctable. The item menu gained Change to pre-order / It arrived — mark as owned — the one thing a silent add can get wrong.

3. The listing page

Two separate listing components existed — one for catalogue-seeded listings, one for user-created ones. The same listing therefore looked different depending on where it was tapped, and the user-created component referenced state it never declared, so a seller opening their own listing hit a blank screen.

ElementBeforeNow
Page identityTwo components; your own listing crashed.Every source normalises into one shape and renders one page — market, collection item, My Space all land identically.
Your order cardQuantity stepper, shipping/local-pickup toggle, estimated total — none of it functional.Removed entirely.
Condition ladderA Sealed / Mint / Like new / Good / Fair / For parts row nobody had entered.Removed — it was leftover seed data contradicting the real category-specific conditions.
Tabs"Details" (mixing catalogue facts with sale terms) and "Seller terms". Shipping appeared in both.Selling terms leads — condition, quantity, ships from, shipping, returns, trades. About the item holds catalogue facts read from the same database entry.
Q&AAlways expanded.Collapsible, with the question count in the header.
Trust rowDeals · Vouches · Replies · Joined — deals aren't tracked anywhere in the product.Vouches · Replies · Joined.

"Is this price fair?" — now functional

Previously three buttons that did nothing. The purpose is narrow: give the seller a signal on whether to adjust the price.

ViewerSeesWhy
VisitorThree options, then a confirmation of their choice with a Change link. No percentages, ever.Published votes would anchor every subsequent buyer's expectation of the price.
SellerA stacked bar and legend (11% / 67% / 22%), a plain-language recommendation, and an Adjust price button.A bar chart alone is not a decision — the card states what to do about it.

A notification fires each time a vote lands on your listing. Votes remain anonymous to the seller — they see the split, never who voted.

4. Copy, naming and removals

  • Market "Saved" filter — was "Saved only" with a trailing count that read like a stepper. Now simply Saved; sort reads "Most saved", results header "3 SAVED".
  • Market condition filter — previously stacked every category's vocabulary at once, making it the longest block in the sheet. Conditions are category-specific, so the chips now appear only after a category is picked. Roughly a third shorter by default.
  • SKU — removed from every user-facing surface (item page, listing page, chat threads). It remains an internal key only.

5. Community access & moderation New

Communities previously had no real gate and no real enforcement — anyone could act inside one, and moderators had no distinct tools from admins. This pass adds an approval gate, a moderation queue, and a permission split.

Design consistency fixes

  • Community card badges now reserve fixed height always, so cards no longer jump size depending on whether a badge is present.
  • Role picker and "Remove member" row now render as matching pills, aligned to the far right.

7. Bugs found while testing this flow

Each of these was found by walking the flow end to end rather than by inspection, and each was a real defect in the build rather than a design question.

Two crashes Blocker

  • Condition chips — options are {id, label, hint} objects and were rendered directly as labels, blanking the screen on any owned item in the finish flow. Condition hints now display as well.
  • Month picker — the month-name arrays were declared inside the calendar component and invisible to other files, so choosing "Month" on a pre-order threw and blanked the app. They now live in shared data.
SymptomCauseFix
"Listed" tag on items with no listingThe tag read a seed flag stored on the shared catalogue row, so it appeared on every collector's copy.One source of truth now drives the tag, the tap target, the filter count and the item page.
Pre-orders showing as for saleThe listing lookup matched on item code alone, and a collection can legitimately hold the same item as both an owned copy and a pre-order — so pre-orders inherited the owned copy's listing.A pre-order is not in hand and can no longer show a listing anywhere.
Wrong copy opening; edits overwriting each otherEverything was keyed by item code, so tapping the pre-order tile opened the owned copy and filling one copy's price patched both.Keyed by individual copy end to end.
Search returning nothing for real itemsThe category filter was pre-seeded from your interests and also applied to search — typing "Pokémon" returned no results.Search spans the whole catalogue unless you set filters yourself.
Quick-add silently refusing itemsThe duplicate guard counted wishlist rows as already owned.Only a real copy blocks a quick-add.
Every pre-order showing "TBD" and counting as incompleteDates are stored structurally but were being read as a plain string.One shared formatter; an explicit "not announced" now counts as an answer rather than a gap.
Your own listing had no listing behind itA seed flag marked the item as listed with no listing record, so the tag, the in-card row and Unlist all resolved to nothing.Real listing records added, including one owned by another collector so the visitor path is exercised.

9. Open for discussion

Four decisions

  • Vocabulary. The heart on listing pages still says "Saved to watchlist" while the filter now says "Saved". Settle on one word.
  • Database link on old listings. Older seeded listings carry no item code, so they don't get the View database entry link. Resolved once every listing is created through the add flow.
  • Seed data realism. The demo collection holds the same Gundam kit as three separate pre-orders. Plausible for a collector, or should the demo data be cleaned?
  • Order date. Currently optional and never asked for. Should the finish flow collect it, or does it stay something only power users fill in?

Suggested next steps

  • Review the pre-order card's expanded state on a narrow screen — six rows plus the listing row is the densest state in the app.
  • Decide whether the pre-order ETA needs day-level precision in the finish flow, or whether month/quarter is sufficient for most collectors.
  • Confirm the two-field completeness bar is the right threshold before the XP and value-privacy rules go live.
