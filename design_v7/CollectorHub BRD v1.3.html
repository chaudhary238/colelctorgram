2Executive Summary

Collecting is a large, fast-growing, and deeply social hobby — yet collectors today are scattered across general-purpose tools never built for them: Instagram for showcasing, WhatsApp and Facebook Groups for community and buy/sell/trade, eBay for transactions, and Discord/Reddit for discussion. No single product combines identity, community, discovery, and trusted peer-to-peer trading tuned to a collector's specific interests.

CollectorHub closes that gap. A collector creates a profile, selects interests, and builds a collection portfolio — adding items from a built-in catalogue (search or barcode scan) and confirming ownership with an in-app photo. Any owned item can be listed for sale or trade in a tap; pre-orders carry a value and timeline. A personalised master feed surfaces new releases, community discussions, marketplace listings, and events. They join communities, message other collectors to transact, confirm completed deals, and build reputation through transaction-linked trust signals — not just a popularity count.

Phase 1 launches with four categories — Action Figures, Designer Toys & Blind Boxes, Model Kits & Lego, and Diecast — targeting 10,000 engaged users. The core bet: collectors return daily if the platform delivers (1) a feed tuned precisely to what they collect, and (2) a trusted place to trade with verifiable peers.

3Product Vision & Objectives

Vision: To be the home for collectors — the single place where any collector can showcase what they own, discover what's new in their niche, find their community, and trade with people they trust.

Mission (Phase 1): Build a focused, high-trust community across four collectible categories that proves collectors will return daily and transact peer-to-peer, before broadening categories and monetisation.

3.1 Strategic Objectives

#ObjectivePhase 1 success looks like
1Reach critical community mass10,000 registered users; a self-sustaining base of daily-active collectors.
2Establish the daily return loopMultiple weekly sessions per active user driven by feed, marketplace, and notifications.

4Shift to user-generated contentDeclining reliance on admin/dummy seeding as organic post share rises.
5Validate category-fitEvidence of which categories drive the most engagement and trade.

3.2 Design Principles

  • Interest-first. Organised around what you collect — a new user with zero followers gets a rich feed on day one.
  • Trust is a feature. Reputation, vouches, and visible seller history are first-class.
  • Seed, then step back. Admin and dummy accounts prime the pump; the product hands the microphone to real users as fast as quality allows.
  • Realistic to build. Phase 1 is intentionally scoped to a shippable MVP; ambitious capabilities deferred to Phase 2.

4Market Context & Opportunity

The collectibles category has moved from niche hobby to mainstream cultural and economic phenomenon. The designer-toy/blind-box secondary market alone was valued at over US$1.8 billion in 2025, propelled by phenomena such as Pop Mart's Labubu. The India toys market was valued at roughly US$2.77 billion in 2025 and the broader collectibles market is forecast to grow at ~10% CAGR through 2033.

Across these markets, collector behaviour is consistent: people want to show off what they own, learn about new releases, find others who share their niche, and buy/sell/trade — increasingly peer-to-peer. Today they stitch this together across Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook Groups, Discord, Reddit, eBay, and category marketplaces. That fragmentation is the opportunity.

4.1 Why Now

  • Mainstreaming of collecting — blind boxes, Lego adult sets (e.g. Lego × Pokémon from 2026), diecast, and designer toys pulling in new adult collectors.
  • Secondary-market momentum — strong resale demand makes a trusted peer marketplace genuinely valuable.
  • Community fragmentation — no dominant collector-native social network exists in the target geographies.
  • Trust deficit — peer trading on Facebook/WhatsApp is rife with scam risk; a reputation/vouch layer is a clear differentiator.

§9.3 Master Feed (Home) — AppBar Layout Correction

Changed in v1.3

The v1.2 spec stated "Create-post top-left · search + notifications top-right." The implementation placed Create and Search both on the left. This is now corrected. The canonical AppBar layout is: Create (left) · Wordmark (centre) · Search + Messages + Bell (right).

Zonev1.2 (incorrect)v1.3 Target (correct)
LeftCreate post + SearchCreate post only
CentreWordmark (squeezed)Wordmark — full ownership of centre
RightMessages + BellSearch + Messages + Bell

Rationale: Search, Messages, and Bell are all discovery & comms actions — grouping them right matches the universal mental model (Instagram, Xiaohongshu/RED, Pinterest). A single Create action left is lighter and lets the wordmark breathe. The current code in Chrome.jsx needs this correction applied.

DSComponent Corrections — v1.3

The following deviations from spec were identified during the v1.3 implementation review. Each must be corrected in app/shared.jsx or app/Cards.jsx.

LocationTag — emoji → Lucide icon

Fix required — shared.jsx

Current implementation uses 📍 {children}. Content guidelines are explicit: no emoji in product copy or component labels. Replace with the Icons.mapPin Lucide icon already in the icon set.

GlassPill — backdrop-filter → flat pill

Fix required — shared.jsx

Current: backdropFilter: 'blur(8px) saturate(180%)'. BRD rule: "No backdrop-filter / glass / blur on the content side." Replace with a flat semi-transparent dark background (rgba(15,23,42,0.65)). Identical visual result; no rule violation. The iOS frame's status bar is an explicit exception and retains its blur.

PostTypeTag — hard-coded hex → token aliases

Fix required — shared.jsx

Current implementation hard-codes hex values ('#2D8F87', '#6B3656', etc.) in the PostTypeTag colour map. These must be replaced with CSS variable references (var(--verified-teal), var(--plum), etc.) so the component follows future token updates automatically.

ActionBtn — inactive colour: --slate-400 → --ink-faint

Fix required — shared.jsx

Current: inactive action buttons (like/comment/share) use var(--slate-400) (#94A3B8 — a cool blue-grey slate). On a white/neutral ink system, inactive UI should use var(--ink-faint) (#999999, warm-neutral grey). The blue-grey reads as slightly tinted against the white background.

StackedAvatars — hard-coded PALETTE → token refs

Fix required — shared.jsx

Current: const PALETTE = ['#FF2442','#8B5CF6','#10B981',...]. These bypass the token system. Replace with token variable references: ['var(--stamp-red)', 'var(--violet)', 'var(--emerald)', 'var(--grail-gold)', 'var(--sky)', 'var(--plum)']. Note: CSS variables cannot be used in a JS array like this directly inside an inline background style — wrap each in a div style attribute or resolve at paint time. Best approach: pass them through the existing Avatar component's color prop, which already handles variable names correctly.

PostCard — dead hover code

Fix required — Cards.jsx

onMouseEnter / onMouseLeave handlers on PostCard apply a translateY(-3px) lift. These never fire on a mobile device. Remove these handlers; keep only pointer-down press scale which applies correctly to touch.

Screen body — missing overscroll-behavior

Fix required — Chrome.jsx

The Screen component's scroll body div is missing overscrollBehavior: 'contain'. On iOS this causes scroll chaining — swiping past top/bottom of the feed bleeds to the outer device frame or bounces unexpectedly. Add it to all scroll containers inside Screen.

Sticky feed header — chrome height

Advisory — FeedView.jsx

Home tab stacks: AppBar (~56px) + 3-tab segmented (~48px) + hashtag scroll bar (~42px) = ~146px of sticky chrome on an 874px frame (17% of screen height). Consider: (a) combining the segmented + hashtag bar into a single row, or (b) collapsing the hashtag bar on scroll. Not blocking but affects perceived content density.

Button — size="block" conflates size and layout

Advisory — shared.jsx

Block-width is a layout concern, not a size variant. A fullWidth boolean prop would keep sizes orthogonal and be more intuitive. Not urgent — current usage is consistent — but worth refactoring before the component is used in more contexts.

13Trust & Safety

13.1 Phase 1 Foundations

  • Transaction-linked trust. Trust built from completed Deals — trade vouches require a confirmed transaction; social endorsements are weighted lower and capped.
  • Ownership verification. Tiered (Claimed / Shown / Verified); in-app-only live camera capture; listing requires Verified. Possession ≠ genuineness.
  • Photo integrity. Perceptual-hash duplicate-image detection and watermarking of verified photos.
  • Reporting & blocking. Report content & users from items, listings, posts, profiles, and DMs.
  • Moderation. Admin moderation queue — review reports, remove content, suspend/ban bad actors.
  • Transaction clarity. Clear guidance that Phase 1 transactions complete off-platform, with safety tips for meet-ups and payments.

13.2 Phase 2 Enhancements

  • Reputation scoring algorithm over the Deal/rating graph; verified/tiered badges.
  • Genuineness verification (item tells, NFC, expert/grading partnerships).
  • Dispute resolution flow and (with in-app payments) escrow protection.
  • Fraud/scam detection and seller KYC for higher-value categories.

17Glossary

TermDefinition
Master FeedThe personalised home stream combining interest- and behaviour-ranked posts, admin content, and joined-community posts.
VouchA peer trust endorsement of a user's profile. Trade vouches require a confirmed Deal; social endorsements are capped and weighted lower.
Collection PortfolioA user's items (owned / wishlist / pre-order) with photos, value, and pre-order timelines.
ItemA catalogue-level collectible record a user owns or wants (the data spine); carries the ownership-verification tier.
ListingA sale/trade offer attached to a collection Item and surfaced in the marketplace — created as an action on the Item, not a post type.
DealA confirmed transaction between two users; the object that unlocks mutual rating and transaction-backed trade-vouch.
Ownership tierAn Item's verification level: Claimed (self-asserted), Shown (in-app live photo), or Verified (passed integrity/challenge checks). Listing requires Verified.
Post typeClassification of a post: Showcase, Discussion, or Review. Selling is an Item action, not a post type.
Return loopThe set of recurring reasons (releases, listings, events, discussions, showcases, notifications) that bring users back daily.
CanvasThe scrollable screen background token (--canvas #F8FAFC) — distinct from --paper (card surfaces). New in v1.3.
Super-userA trusted/high-reputation user who may receive elevated privileges (e.g. event approval waiver).
